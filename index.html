Part 1

Judging process

1.1Purpose

The Pit Showcase is here to help guide your team through the rest of the season. The interview and the feedback you get afterward are meant to show you what to focus on next, not to rank you against other teams.

1.2Judging principles

Everyone is new to this

Judges are experiencing FTC for the first time too. Nothing here assumes game-specific expertise — only what any first-time judge can see, ask about and understand.

Evidence over polish

Sketches, calculations, photos and logs count. A hand-drawn design with sound reasoning can beat a polished CAD with none.

Show it, don't promise it — gently

With only about a week to prepare, a clear and specific plan can count alongside work already done. It doesn't need to be finished — just genuine and thought through, not vague.

Judge the journey

Scores reflect what a team did with the time, people and resources it had — including a short build window.

Share the floor

More than one team member should be able to speak for the team across the interview — not just a single spokesperson.

Encourage first

Judges ask, listen and guide. This is a practice run to help teams learn — not a contest to find fault.

1.3Judging flow

Event schedule

TimeStageWhat happens
10:00-12:00Team interviewsOne 2-judge panel per team, about 15 minutes each. A single comprehensive conversation covers Engineering Excellence and Team Impact together — there is no separate round.
2:00-3:00DeliberationJudges agree on Engineering Excellence, Team Impact and the three Judges' Awards, then confirm Best Overall.

1.4Documentation

Each team submits a 1-page poster and an Engineering Portfolio (maximum 3 pages) before the event. Judges read both before the interview and use them throughout judging. Anything beyond 3 pages in the Portfolio will not be read or considered.

1-page poster

SectionWhat to include
Team and journeyHow the team formed and what has happened so far.
RolesWho does what.

Engineering Portfolio (max 3 pages)

SectionWhat to include
Robot and planRobot design highlights and a simple plan for scoring points. No prior FTC strategy experience is expected.
OutreachWhat the team has done to reach people outside the team, with any proof.
Funding and next stepsBudget, sponsor or school support if any, and a simple idea for continuing next season.
Note: Optional but encouraged. Judges don't score the Portfolio by itself, and neat presentation earns nothing extra — clear, honest content is what helps.

2.2Award criteria

AwardFTC equivalentWinnersScoring
2.2.1Engineering Excellence Innovate + Control + Think1st, 2nd, 3rd of 1315 points

For the team with the clearest engineering thinking, from robot design to a simple plan for scoring points. No working robot is needed, and no prior FTC game experience is assumed of judges or teams. A single subsystem or chassis is enough if the reasoning is sound.

Engineering Excellence criteria (15 points)

Engineering Excellence Criteria
Required1The team can point to a specific part of the robot they designed or built, and explain why they made it that way.0-3
Required2They tried more than one idea for at least one part of the robot, and explain why they kept the one they did.0-3
Encouraged3They tested the robot (or a part or prototype of it) and can describe something they changed because of a test.0-3
Encouraged4They have a simple plan for how the robot will try to score points in a match — it doesn't need to be optimal, just thought through.0-3
Encouraged5They can show the robot, a sketch or a CAD file that matches what they're describing.0-3
2.2.2Team Impact Connect + Reach + Sustain1st, 2nd, 3rd of 1315 points

For the team that has made a genuine first effort to connect with people outside the team, with a simple idea for keeping it going. Given the short time before this event, a small, honest effort counts as much as a big one.

Team Impact criteria (15 points)

Team Impact Criteria
Required1The team can describe at least one way they tried to reach people outside the team (their school, community or sponsors), and how.0-3
Encouraged2They have some simple proof of what they did — a photo, message or sign-in sheet is enough; it does not need to be polished.0-3
Encouraged3The team is structured for this work: roles are clear, with someone responsible for outreach and community connection.0-3
Encouraged4They can describe a next step for outreach they're already planning, even before it happens.0-3
Encouraged5The outreach connects to the team's own journey — who they are and why it mattered to them, matching their poster.0-3
Note: It's fine if this is small — a genuine first step counts. Judges are here to encourage, not to grade a finished program.
2.2.3Judges' Awards 3 awards, judges' choiceConsensus

Three awards the judging panel gives at its own discretion, for anything the rubrics above don't fully capture. Judges name each award on the day and choose the team by consensus — there is no fixed rubric or points table.

Judges' Awards — things judges may consider

ConsiderationFor example
GrowthHow far the team came from where they started, given their time and resources.
TeamworkHow the team handled questions together, under a little pressure.
CreativityAn interesting idea, pit design or way of telling their story.
SpiritWarmth, welcome and enthusiasm at the pit and in the interview.
Note: A Judges' Award can go to any team, including one that also won Engineering Excellence, Team Impact or Best Overall.
2.2.4Best Overall Team Inspire1 winner, all teamsAward 1 + Award 2

The team that stands out across both Engineering Excellence and Team Impact. Best Overall is not a separately scored category — it is the combination of Award 1 (Engineering Excellence) and Award 2 (Team Impact), which judges review together and confirm by consensus.

Best Overall Team — discussion points

Best Overall Team Discussion Points
Required1The team has a strong combined standing across Engineering Excellence and Team Impact.
Encouraged2Every member can explain both the robot idea and the outreach story in their own words.
Encouraged3Roles are shared; the team works and communicates as one.
Disclaimer: People's Choice is a separate, individual award given to every team and is not considered for Best Overall.
2.2.5People's Choice MotivateEvery team, individuallyMentor's pick

Every team receives its own People's Choice recognition — this is not a single event winner. Each team's mentor picks one thing they'd like to celebrate about their own team. There is no competition here: every team gets one.

People's Choice — how it works

StepWhat happens
NominationThe team's mentor picks one thing to celebrate about their own team — spirit, creativity, kindness, teamwork, improvement, or anything else. Mentor's choice.
CollectionSubmitted at the pit or to an organiser during lunch and pit visiting (by 2:00 pm).
PresentedRead out for every team at the ceremony, alongside the judged awards.
Disclaimer: People's Choice is not considered for Best Overall or any other award.
