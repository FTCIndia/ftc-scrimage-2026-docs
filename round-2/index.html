Part 1

Judging process

1.1Purpose and what is new this round

This round of the Pit Showcase is built to get teams ready for real FTC judging: the portfolio and the way teams talk with judges. It keeps the same encouraging spirit as last time and asks for a little more.

What is different from the first Pit Showcase:

A fuller Engineering Portfolio

Teams may now build a longer portfolio, and it can continue last round's notebook. See section 1.2.

Criteria from real FTC awards

Each criterion is tied to a real FTC judged award (Innovate, Design, Control, Connect, Reach, Sustain, and Think), so teams learn what each one looks for.

Concrete items

Each area includes measurable things a team has built or done, such as a chassis that drives or money raised, scored on the same 0 to 3 scale.

1.2Engineering Portfolio

Teams may submit a cover page plus up to 8 pages of content, and the portfolio can be a continuation of the one from the first Pit Showcase. Real FTC allows up to 15 pages of content, so 8 pages is a stepping stone toward the full notebook.

Continuing last round's portfolio. Teams are encouraged to build on what they already wrote. Keep pages that are still true, and add new pages for new work. Mark every page added since the last Showcase with a small "New" tag, and open the first content page with two lines on what changed since last time. Old pages count toward the 8-page limit, so condense older entries to make room.

The Documentation award reads the whole portfolio, but scores only how clearly it is written and organized, not how good the robot is.

1.3Scoring scale

Each criterion is scored 0 to 3 based on evidence, and the same scale is used for every criterion in every award.

Scoring scale, applied to every scored criterion

ScoreMeaningWhat the judge saw or heard
0Not shownNothing in the portfolio or in conversation with judges
1StatedThe team says it, but there is no evidence yet
2ShownEvidence in the portfolio or at the robot, but the team does not yet explain why or what they learned
3ExplainedClear evidence, and the team explains what they tried, what they learned, or what comes next

How scores add up

TopicRule
TotalsEach area has 4 criteria, so each area is worth 12 points. Best Overall adds the three areas for a total of 36.
Criteria with bulletsGive one score for the whole criterion, based on how many of its bullets the team showed: 0 for none, 1 for a few, 2 for most, and 3 for all, with the team explaining why it matters.
Required and EncouragedRequired criteria are what every team is expected to show, as at FTC. Encouraged criteria are where stronger teams stand out. A team that shows few Encouraged items can still score well.
TiesJudges settle ties in deliberation by comparing Required criteria first.

1.4How teams should prepare

The best preparation is to practice the same things FTC judging asks for: a short talk, a portfolio that backs it up, and a robot you can show and explain.

Plan the talk

Several students should share it.

  1. Who we are, our roles, and our goals this round
  2. The robot: one mechanism, the options we considered, and a test that changed something
  3. Outreach: what we did, who it reached, and what happened
  4. Our plan for the team going forward

Get the portfolio ready

  • Keep it within the 8-page limit and bring a printed copy for judges.
  • Date every entry and write down why you decided things, not only what you did.
  • Mark new pages if you are continuing last round's portfolio.

Be ready to show the robot

  • Have the robot powered and ready to show one working function.
  • Let the students who built a part explain it.
  • Be honest about what is not working yet and what you plan to try next. Judges respond well to teams that know their robot's weak points.

2.2.1Engineering Excellence Innovate + Design + ControlTop 312 points

Engineering Excellence rewards teams that can explain and show how their robot is designed, controlled, and improved. It covers the same ground as three FTC awards: Innovate (a creative solution), Design (a well-balanced robot), and Control (sensors and software). Teams can explain things in plain words; no game-specific jargon is needed.

Engineering Excellence criteria (12 points)

#CriterionWhat the team showsFTC awardType
1Robot worksThe robot is built and does the basics:
  • The chassis (the drive base) is built and drives
  • At least one mechanism picks up, holds, or moves a game piece
  • At least one autonomous action, even if it is only driving forward
  • Two or more students can each drive it
Design, ControlRequired
2Creative idea and risksThe team points to one new or different feature of the robot and explains:
  • The problem it solves and why they tried it
  • One way it could fail, and what they did to make it more reliable
InnovateRequired
3Design reasonsThe team explains why the robot looks the way it does:
  • What it needs to do, and what they gave up to get there
  • How it is easy to build or repair, such as labeled parts, spare parts, or quick-swap pieces
DesignRequired
4Control and consistencyThe team names one sensor or software feature in plain words and says how often their autonomous or driver controls work:
  • The problem the sensor or software solves
  • How many times they tried it, how often it worked, and what they changed
ControlEncouraged
What this award does not score. How the design work is recorded in the portfolio is scored under Documentation. Outreach, roles, and planning are scored under Team Impact.
2.2.2Team Impact Connect + Reach + SustainTop 312 points

Team Impact rewards teams that grow their skills, connect with their community, and plan to keep going. It covers the same ground as three FTC awards: Connect (partnerships with the STEM community), Reach (introducing new people to FIRST), and Sustain (keeping the team going).

Team Impact criteria (12 points)

#CriterionWhat the team showsFTC awardType
1Learning and connectionsThe team shows how they are growing their skills:
  • Written goals for what members want to learn, and the steps they are taking
  • A talk with a STEM professional, a mentor, or another team, and what they learned or changed afterward
ConnectRequired
2Outreach done, with proofAt least one outreach activity is completed:
  • Who they wanted to reach, why, and how they chose the activity
  • Date, place, and number of people reached, with proof such as a photo, sign-in sheet, or thank-you note
  • Optional: someone new got involved because of it, such as a member, volunteer, mentor, or team
ReachRequired
3Money and budgetThe team has a simple written budget and has raised or received money:
  • A list of the season's costs
  • The amount raised and where it came from, shown with a receipt, sponsor letter, or simple ledger
SustainRequired
4A team that lastsThe team is set up to keep going:
  • A named owner for each big job (building, programming, documentation, outreach) and who is learning each role next
  • A plan to recruit new members and carry on next season
  • A written regular meeting schedule
SustainEncouraged
What this award does not score. Robot design and control belong to Engineering Excellence. How the outreach pages are written and laid out belongs to Documentation.
2.2.3Documentation ThinkTop 312 points

The Documentation award rewards teams whose portfolio is clear, organized, and tells the story of their season. It is scored from the portfolio alone and matches the Documentation area in FTC (the Think Award). It scores how the work is recorded, not how good the robot or the outreach is.

What this award does not score. Only what is written in the portfolio counts here. What the team says about the robot or outreach in person is scored under Engineering Excellence and Team Impact. Page and file limits are event rules, not scored.
2.2.4Best Overall Team Inspire1 winnerOut of 36

The team that stands out across all three areas. Best Overall is not scored separately: it is the highest combined total of Engineering Excellence, Team Impact, and Documentation (out of 36). Like the Inspire Award at FTC, it goes to a team that is positive and inclusive, with every member contributing.

2.2.5Judges' Awards Judges' Choice3 awardsConsensus

Awards the judging panel gives at its own discretion, for something the rubrics do not capture, such as resilience, teamwork, or a standout moment. Judges name each award on the day and choose the team by consensus; there is no rubric or points table.
